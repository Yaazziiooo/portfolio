About the Game

Sub-Optimal is a lighthearted online multiplayer game where divers navigate underwater hazards and hostile sea creatures while searching for treasure.

Developed in Unreal Engine 5, the game combines cooperative gameplay with different enemy behaviors and dynamic underwater challenges.

My Contributions

As a Gameplay & AI Programmer, I focused on developing enemy behaviors, damage systems, and networked gameplay functionality using C++.

Enemy AI Systems

Designed and implemented AI behaviors for sharks, squids, and jellyfish, including patrol, detection, and attack mechanics.

Finite State Machine

Developed a state-based AI system for the shark, handling transitions between patrolling, chasing, attacking, and returning to patrol.

Spline-Based Movement

Implemented enemy patrol movement using Unreal Engine's USplineComponent to support smooth movement along configurable paths.

Damage Systems

Developed gameplay damage functionality, including timed damage behavior for jellyfish encounters.

Multiplayer Replication

Implemented server-authoritative AI behavior and replicated gameplay functionality to synchronize enemy interactions across connected clients.

Modular Enemy Architecture

Built a reusable AEnemyBase class for shared functionality such as health and replication. This provided a common foundation for different enemy types.

Technical Highlights

Server-Authoritative AI

One of the main challenges was maintaining consistent enemy behavior in an online multiplayer environment.

I used Unreal Engine's authority checks and replication framework to keep AI behavior synchronized between the server and connected clients. Debugging replication issues helped me improve the reliability of the enemy systems.

Reusable AI Architecture

To avoid duplicating functionality, I structured the enemy classes around a shared AEnemyBase implementation. Common features such as health and replication could be reused, while individual enemies implemented their own behaviors.

This approach improved consistency and made enemy configurations easier to adjust during development.
